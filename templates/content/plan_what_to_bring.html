Preparation & Packing Checklist

What to Bring on a Lake Naivasha Boat Safari

Being well prepared on the water makes all the difference between feeling chilled and squinting in the sun versus enjoying an unhurried, comfortable wilderness experience. Here is the captain-approved checklist for your morning or afternoon ride.

Sun Protection

Water reflects tropical UV rays intensely. Bring a wide-brim hat (preferably with a chin strap against lake breezes), UV-blocking sunglasses, and broad-spectrum sunscreen.

Warm Windbreaker Layer

At 1,884m elevation, early mornings on Lake Naivasha are crisp (12°C–15°C). Bring a lightweight fleece or windbreaker jacket that you can easily peel off as the sun warms the water.

Closed-Toe Walking Shoes

If you plan to disembark on Crescent Island, comfortable trainers or hiking sneakers are strongly recommended. Trails are flat but cross dirt, dry grass, and occasional acacia thorns.

Optics & Camera Gear

Bring 8x42 binoculars for raptors and shorebirds. For photography, a 70–300mm or 100–400mm lens allows framing fish eagle dives and hippo yawn displays from ethical safety distances.

Water & Dry Bag

Carry a reusable water bottle to stay hydrated under high-altitude sun. A water-resistant backpack or dry sack protects electronics from unexpected spray during open water transits.

M-Pesa / Cash for Entry

If heading to Crescent Island, keep funds ready on M-Pesa or Visa/MasterCard. The sanctuary ranger gate at the boat landing accepts electronic payments for the official gate entry fee.

What You Should Leave in Your Vehicle

  • Bulky Hard-Shell Luggage: Keep large suitcases in your vehicle trunk; boat decks have limited floor clearance.
  • Drones / UAVs: Flying drones over Lake Naivasha and Crescent Island is strictly prohibited by Kenya Wildlife Service regulations without special clearance.
  • Loud Bluetooth Speakers: To respect the lake ecosystem and wild hippo pods, amplified music is not allowed on safari rides.
