The Natural Wonder of Lake Naivasha

Sitting at the highest elevation of the Great Rift Valley, Lake Naivasha is a unique freshwater ecosystem spanning roughly 139 square kilometers. Surrounded by dormant volcanoes, papyrus swamps, and yellow-fever acacia woodlands, it hosts one of East Africa’s densest hippo populations and over 400 recorded bird species.

A Living Great Rift Valley Ecosystem

Unlike the alkaline soda lakes of the Rift Valley (such as Lake Nakuru or Lake Bogoria), Lake Naivasha is a freshwater lake fed by the perennial Malewa and Gilgil rivers flowing down from the Aberdare mountain range. Because it has no visible surface outlet, it is believed to drain via underground volcanic fissures into subterranean aquifers.

The water level fluctuates dramatically across multi-year cycles. Over recent years, rising water levels have created new submerged acacia forests that provide pristine perching sites for fish eagles and shelter for juvenile tilapia, black bass, and common carp.
